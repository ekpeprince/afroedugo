## Overview: Employment Rights for International Students in Lithuania

Lithuania offers one of the most welcoming student employment frameworks in the European Union. Unlike countries that require arduous employer-sponsored labor market testing or separate work permits, Lithuania automatically grants working rights to degree-seeking international students.

Whether you wish to offset living costs, gain European professional experience, or build local networks for future permanent residence, this guide details your legal rights and tax obligations.

---

## 1. Statutory Working Hours Regulations

Lithuania's Labor Code and immigration laws establish clear parameters for student employment:

| Academic Period | Permitted Working Hours | Conditions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **During Semester (Term Time)** | Up to **20 hours per week** | Must be enrolled as a full-time degree student in an accredited institution. Work hours must not conflict with mandatory lectures or lab sessions. |
| **Official Summer Break (July & August)** | Up to **40 hours per week** (Full-time) | Permitted during official summer vacation as specified in the university academic calendar. |
| **Winter / Inter-Semester Break** | Up to **40 hours per week** (Full-time) | Permitted during official inter-semester holiday periods. |
| **Master's & Doctoral Candidates** | Up to **40 hours per week** (Full-time) | Master's degree and PhD students are entitled to full-time employment rights under current Lithuanian migration amendments. |

## 2. Tax Registration & Setting Up Your VMI Account

Before beginning formal employment, your employer will register you with **Sodra (State Social Insurance Fund)**. You will also interact with **VMI (State Tax Inspectorate — Valstybinė mokesčių inspekcija)**:

1. **Personal Identification Code (Asmens kodas)**: Your unique Lithuanian national ID code is printed directly on your TRP card. This code is used for all payroll, social security, and tax filings.
2. **Personal Income Tax (GPM)**: Standard income tax rate is 20%. Students earning near minimum wage benefit from the Non-Taxable Income Allowance (**NPD — Neapmokestinamasis pajamų dydis**), meaning the effective tax on a part-time student wage is low.
3. **Health & Social Insurance (PSD & VSD)**: Automatic deductions from your paycheck cover compulsory healthcare coverage through Sodra, giving you access to state polyclinics and hospitals.

## 3. High-Demand Student Job Sectors in Lithuania

Because Lithuanian universities offer internationally recognized degrees in technology, engineering, and business, several high-demand sectors actively recruit international students:

### A. Shared Service Centers & International Tech Companies
Vilnius and Kaunas host major international hubs (such as Western Union, Cognizant, Telia, Transcom, and Booking.com).
- **Typical Roles**: Multilingual Customer Operations, IT Support Desk, Quality Assurance, Content Moderation.
- **Languages Valued**: English, French, Spanish, Arabic, German, and Portuguese.
- **Pay Scale**: €7.50 – €12.00 / hour.

### B. Logistics, Hospitality & Delivery
- **Typical Roles**: Food delivery (Wolt, Bolt), warehouse operations, cafes, and event catering.
- **Flexibility**: Highly flexible shift schedules that fit around morning or afternoon lecture blocks.
- **Pay Scale**: €6.50 – €9.00 / hour.

## 4. Post-Graduation Job Seeker Residence Permit (12 Months)

Upon successful defense of your bachelor's or master's thesis and receipt of your diploma:

1. You are legally entitled to apply for a **12-Month Job Seeker Temporary Residence Permit**.
2. This permit grants full Schengen residency while you interview for qualified positions across Lithuania and the EU.
3. Once an employer offers you a qualified contract, you transition directly into an **EU Blue Card** or a standard Highly Skilled Worker TRP with a fast-track route to permanent EU residency.
